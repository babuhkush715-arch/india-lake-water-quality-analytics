# India Lake Water Quality Analytics

End-to-end analytics project on CPCB NWMP lake water quality data (2017-2022): Python ETL, SQLite and SQL analysis, CPCB class screening, and a Power BI dashboard (in progress).

## Data source
CPCB National Water Quality Monitoring Programme (NWMP), via the Kaggle dataset
["NWMP Water Quality Data for Indian Lakes"](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/akkshaysr/nwmp-water-quality-data-for-indian-lakes).
The Kaggle page lists the license as "Unknown", and the original source is the CPCB website (cpcb.nic.in). For that reason the raw data and the tables derived from it are not included in this repo. Everything is regenerated by the pipeline; see "How to run".

## Pipeline
Raw CSV (6 yearly files) -> Python ETL (cleaning and validation) -> SQLite -> SQL analysis (shared classification view) -> classified CSV -> Power BI dashboard

## Findings so far
- 699 stations across 31 states/UTs. Karnataka (171) and Telangana (160) make up about half.
- The monitoring network grew from 373 stations (2017) to 639 (2022). Only 321 stations have data for all 6 years, so year-on-year comparisons use percentages and a fixed 321-station panel, not raw counts. Much of the growth is in a few states: 127 of Telangana's 160 stations and 77 of Karnataka's 171 were added after 2017.
- The 10 stations with the highest fecal coliform maxima are all in Karnataka.
- On DO and BOD alone, the share of station-years not meeting CPCB Class A-C fell from about 70% (2017) to about 63% (2022) in the fixed panel, but looked flat (67-73%) across the whole network. In 2022, stations monitored for fewer than 6 years had a higher share (77%) than the panel (63%), which may explain why the network-wide share looks flat.
- With the full CPCB Class A-C criteria (DO, BOD, pH, total coliform), the share of station-years that meet them in the fixed panel rose from 22-25% (2017) to 32-35% (2022), with a dip in 2018. The range comes from station-years where pH or coliform is missing. The rise also appears without Karnataka (234 stations: 28-31% to 41-44%), while Karnataka's 87 panel stations stay low (about 5-12%, small counts).
- In 2022, 8 of 639 station-years (1.3%) met full Class A criteria, versus 31 (4.9%) on DO/BOD alone. Adding pH and coliform moved 16 of those 31 and 52 of the 116 DO/BOD Class B station-years into a lower class.
- No significance testing was done and causes were not analysed.

## Method: CPCB class screening
Thresholds from the CPCB [Designated Best Use Water Quality Criteria](https://cpcb.nic.in/wqm/Designated_Best_Use_Water_Quality_Criteria.pdf) (checked 2026-10-12):

| Class | DO (mg/L) | BOD (mg/L) | pH | Total coliform (MPN/100ml) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 6 or more | 2 or less | 6.5-8.5 | 50 or less |
| B | 5 or more | 3 or less | 6.5-8.5 | 500 or less |
| C | 4 or more | 3 or less | 6.0-9.0 | 5,000 or less |

- Worst-case annual values are used: minimum DO, maximum BOD, pH minimum and maximum, maximum total coliform.
- Only Classes A-C are assessed. Classes D and E need ammonia, conductivity and SAR, which are not in this data, so "Below C" means "does not meet A-C".
- Missing and BDL values are not treated as clean water. They give "Insufficient data" unless DO/BOD already fails.

## Data quality notes
- 40 junk header rows removed during ETL; 10 physically implausible values removed (for example DO above 25 mg/L).
- 135 "BDL" (below detection limit) values treated as missing, 80 of them total coliform.
- 8 station-year records have fecal coliform above 10^8 MPN/100ml (2021-2022, all Karnataka). They are kept but flagged, not verified against the source PDFs.
- Coliform values repeat in fixed steps (for example 540, 1,600, 160,000), which looks like a lab-method effect; not independently verified.
- 15 year-over-year spikes (more than 100x, previous value at least 1,000) found, 13 of them in Karnataka; values not verified against the source.

## Limitations
- Maharashtra has no stations in this dataset.
- Data is annual min/max per station, not a time series.
- No GPS coordinates, so maps will be state-level.
- Class screening covers A-C only, uses worst-case annual values, and treats BDL as missing, which may slightly understate the share of cleaner classes.
- The fixed panel has 87 Karnataka stations out of 321, and Karnataka's results differ from the rest (see findings).

etl/           clean_lakes.py

`data/raw/`, `data/clean/`, `data/export/` and `data/lakes.db` are not tracked in git; they are recreated by the steps below.

## How to run
1. `pip install -r requirements.txt`
2. Download the six yearly CSVs from the Kaggle dataset above, rename them to `2017_lake_data.csv` ... `2022_lake_data.csv`, and place them in `data/raw/`.
3. `python etl/clean_lakes.py` (creates `data/clean/*.csv` and `data/lakes.db`)
4. Open `data/lakes.db` in DB Browser for SQLite. Run the CREATE VIEW block in `sql/01_basic.sql` once, press Ctrl+S, then run individual queries. Each query has an "Expected" line to check your result.
5. To rebuild the Power BI input, run `sql/02_export_powerbi.sql` and export the result to `data/export/fact_lake_quality_classified.csv`.
